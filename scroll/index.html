Pediatric dentistry

What a child’s visit is for.

01

The first look

A first visit is short. Your child can sit with you. We count teeth, look at the gums, and show you how to brush a mouth that will not hold still. The usual recommendation is a first look by the first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth.

02

Prevention, kept brief

Checkups, a cleaning the child can finish, fluoride varnish, and sealants on the grooves of new molars. We talk about snacks, cups, and habits while the appointment is still easy to sit through.

03

Only as far as the tooth needs

When a baby tooth needs a filling, pulp care, or a space maintainer, we say what we are doing before we begin. A visit can stop. Coming back another day is part of the plan.

The care

Six parts of a children’s practice.

The first visit

Short, with a parent close by. We look, name what we see, and stop while the child is still willing.

Checkups and cleaning

A look for early decay, a polish, and fluoride varnish. Most of these visits end before patience runs out.

Sealants

A thin coat on the grooves of back teeth, where a brush does not reach well. Painted on, then left alone.

Fillings

Tooth-colored fillings for baby teeth. Each step is explained in plain words before it starts.

Holding space

If a baby tooth is lost early, a space maintainer keeps room for the adult tooth that is still to come.

A tooth that hurts

A toothache, a chip, or a knock. Call first. We will say whether it should be the same day.

Also in the practice: fluoride at routine visits, pulp care when decay in a baby tooth is deep, and guidance when a thumb, a pacifier, or a growing bite needs watching.

Before you come.

Tell your child you are going to the dentist to count teeth. Skip promises that nothing will be felt, and skip stories about your own difficult appointments. A comfort object is welcome.

Bring notes on medicines, medical conditions, and any words your child already uses for mouth, teeth, or pain. If the morning of the visit is a hard one, call and move the day. A parent or guardian stays in the room.
