Services

Care for primary teeth, and the adult teeth arriving behind them.

The practice is built around pediatric dentistry. Adult family members are referred to a colleague when they need their own dentist.

01

Infant and first visits

A lap or chair exam, a count of teeth, and coaching for brushing at home. The goal of the first visit is a calm look, not a long procedure.

02

Examinations and cleanings

Routine visits check growth, gums, and early decay. Cleaning and fluoride varnish are paced so a child can finish them.

03

Sealants

A protective coat for the grooves of permanent molars, placed once those teeth have erupted far enough to keep dry.

04

Fillings for baby teeth

Tooth-colored restorations when decay is limited. Baby teeth that still have years of work to do are worth repairing.

05

Pulp treatment

When decay is deep but the tooth should stay until it loosens on its own. Dr. Reyes plans this care and explains the steps first.

06

Space maintainers

If a baby tooth is lost early, a small appliance holds room for the permanent tooth so the neighbors do not drift.

07

Dental injuries

Chips, loosened teeth, and knocks. Call the same day. A baby tooth that has come all the way out is not put back in. We still need to see the child.

08

Habits and growth

Thumb and pacifier habits, crowding, and the early bite. We watch, and we refer to an orthodontist when the timing is right.

Patients

Who we see

Infants

From the first tooth. A parent holds the child. We look, and we send you home with a way to clean the mouth tonight.

Children

Checkups, sealants, and treatment for decay or a toothache. Visits stay as short as the tooth allows.

Teens

Prevention through the arrival of permanent teeth, plus an early look at the bite. Some teens need a longer visit. We plan for that.

New patients

What the first visit actually is.

  1. 01
    You write or call

    Tell us the child’s age, any pain, and whether they have been to a dentist before.

  2. 02
    We ask a few things

    Medicines, medical conditions, and the words your child uses for teeth. If the day arrives and it is a bad morning, move it.

  3. 03
    We look first

    A parent stays. Cleaning or treatment happens only if the child can manage it in that sitting.

  4. 04
    You leave with a plan

    A return in six months, help with brushing, or a second visit to finish care. Nothing restorative starts without a fee and a yes.

For parents

Before the appointment.

Say that the dentist will count teeth. Leave out promises that nothing will be felt, and leave out stories of your own bad visits.

When should the first visit be?

By the first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth, whichever comes first. An older child who has never been should still come.

Do I stay in the room?

Yes. A parent or guardian stays for the exam and for treatment.

What if my child will not open?

Then that is the visit. We stop. Forcing a first appointment makes the next one harder, so we schedule a shorter return.

What if a tooth is knocked or chipped?

Call the same day. Keep a knocked-out permanent tooth moist, in milk if you have it, and bring it with you. A baby tooth is not placed back into the socket. The child should still be seen.
